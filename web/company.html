The bottleneck

It is not compute.
It never was.

The corpus does not exist

You cannot scrape contact forces, grip adjustments or the half second of hesitation before a fragile object. None of it was ever written down, so none of it can be crawled.

Lab data does not generalise

Policies trained on rigs in controlled rooms fall over in real kitchens. The variance that breaks them is exactly the variance a lab removes on purpose.

Collection does not scale on payroll

Teleoperation at 30 dollars an hour puts a hard ceiling on how much experience anyone can buy. The ceiling is economic, not technical.

The network

Contributors own
what they record

Data collection normally means a company keeps the asset and the person who made it gets an hourly rate. That is why supply caps out. Invert it and supply compounds.

Contribution is a record, not a receipt

Every accepted clip is written to Avalanche with its licence terms, the contributor, and a hash of the payload. Chosen for cheap finality and subsecond settlement, because a network paying out per clip cannot carry mainnet gas.

Payment follows usage, not upload

Contributors earn when a buyer licenses a slice their data sits in. It aligns the incentive with quality: volume alone earns nothing if nobody trains on it.

Buyers get provenance, not promises

Every hour licensed carries a verifiable chain back to the person who recorded it and the terms they agreed. That is the part a legal team actually blocks on, and it is the hardest thing for a competitor to retrofit.

Where this honestly stands. Hotaru is real and shipping, and its CAD, STLs and audits are public today. The wearables are in design. The network and the settlement layer are the model we are building toward, not something you can use this week. We would rather say that here than have you find out after signing something.
